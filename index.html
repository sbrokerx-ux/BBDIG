Other strategies cities use

TIF is one tool among many. Cities use a range of approaches to attract and retain businesses in their neighborhoods, and we can help you evaluate which fit.

  • Special Service Areas (SSAs) and Business Improvement Districts (BIDs)

    Local business or property owners agree to a special tax assessment on themselves to fund clean streets, coordinated marketing, security, and district management.

  • Façade improvement grants

    Direct grants or low-interest loans from the city that help storefronts upgrade their signs, windows, and exterior appearance.

  • Enterprise zones and economic incentive zones

    State or locally designated areas offering tax credits, sales tax exemptions on building materials, or utility discounts for job-creating businesses.

  • Small business technical assistance and incubators

    Free legal, financial, or marketing training, plus affordable shared commercial space and pop-up retail opportunities.

  • Streamlined permitting and zoning relief

    Expedited plan reviews, fee waivers, and flexible zoning rules such as mixed-use overlays, making it easier and faster for businesses to open or expand.

Why BBDIG exists

BBDIG, the Brown Business Development & Investment Group, was founded on a simple belief: growth a community creates should be reinvested in that community.

Tax Increment Financing is one of the few tools that does this directly, but it only works when the district is well formed, the grant funding is secured, and the increment is tracked year over year. That oversight is where we focus: grants only, no bonds, no outside investors.

We use AI to do that work faster and more thoroughly, and we keep people responsible for every decision. Our work stays local and specific: the districts, projects, and municipal partners that make up cities' economies.
